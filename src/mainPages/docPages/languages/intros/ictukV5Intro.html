Basics

Letters: 3 Key Attributes

There are 3 attributes to letters that will come up everywhere in this guide, and 2 in Ictuk overall: their sound, their glyph, and their roman transliteration. The roman-equivalent does not apply to the world Ictuk is spoken in until much farther into the future, but it is a useful medium for scenarios in which glyphs are difficult to reproduce or show. Illegal Sounds

The only illegal sound in Ictuk is when the same consonant is next to another. Instead, only one of the consonants gets pronounced. For example, "tt" is just pronounced as "t".

Writing

Glyph Structures

You may have noticed from the previous exercise, but all letter glyphs stem from zh, meaning every glyph's first stroke should be zh.

Some glyphs can be thin from the front, back, top, or bottom, they can be shorter than other glyphs, or they can be slanted in the top or bottom. All of these properties affect how they couple with other glyphs. The entire goal is the compress glyphs as much as possible while still retaining the meaning of the word or phrase.
Kofafashtuf ("I am good").

Words

Ictuk words can represent single English words to whole English phrases, but they are still seperated by spaces.
Fotash shap ("Hello there")
